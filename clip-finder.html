01 · How it works

A 3-hour stream has a handful of moments worth posting. Finding them is the slow part. Clip Finder gets Claude to read the whole stream for you and hand back the best moments, with timestamps and titles.

  1. Get the stream's transcript (below).
  2. Paste Prompt 1 into Claude, then the transcript. Get your top 5 moments.
  3. Use Prompts 2 and 3 for stronger titles and the post caption.
  4. Cut, reframe and caption each clip in your editor.

Getting a transcript

  • YouTube streams and videos (easiest): open the video → click …more under the title → Show transcript → select all the text in the panel and copy it. Keep the timestamps in.
  • Twitch VODs: Twitch doesn't show transcripts. Many streamers upload their VODs to a YouTube channel, so use those. Otherwise you need a transcription tool.
  • Your own recordings: any transcription tool that keeps timestamps works.

Long streams

If the transcript is very long, paste it in parts (for example one hour at a time) and run Prompt 1 on each part. Then ask Claude: "Of all the moments you found, which 5 are the strongest?"

What a transcript can't tell you: it only has what was said. It can't hear a scream, see a clutch play or read chat. Always watch each moment before you cut it.

02 · Prompt 1: The Moment Finder

Paste this into Claude first, then paste the transcript straight after it.

You're an editor for a short-form clipping channel. I'm going to paste the transcript of a livestream, with timestamps. Find the 5 best moments to post as vertical clips on TikTok, Reels and Shorts.

A good moment works on its own, with no context: a reaction with a clear cause, a punchline, a fail, a clutch play, a story with an ending, or a hot take. Loud isn't the same as good. Skip anything that needs the viewer to know what happened earlier.

For each moment, give me:
1. Start and end time. Start a few seconds before the setup and end just after the payoff. Aim for 15 to 45 seconds; let the story decide the length.
2. What happens, in one line.
3. Why it works as a clip, in one line.
4. The first 3 seconds: what the clip should open on (usually the biggest reaction or the payoff).
5. A hook title: 2 to 6 words, in capitals, that makes people want the ending without giving it away. Never claim something that didn't happen.
6. A spoiler warning if the moment gives away a story or plot point.

Rank them strongest first. Then list up to 3 runners-up with just the times and one line each.

If the transcript is cut off or too long to read fully, tell me which part you covered.

04 · Clipping programs on one page

A clipping program is a creator (or their team) paying people to post short clips of their streams. You post clips on your own accounts, submit the links, and get paid by views. Platforms like Whop host many of them as "content rewards"; some creators run their own on Discord.

The usual rules (every campaign differs)

  • Only the listed creator's content, sometimes only recent streams.
  • Credit: their name or tag in the caption, sometimes a required hashtag or sound.
  • Quality: vertical, captions, no other clipper's watermark, no reposting other people's clips.
  • Real views only: bought or botted views get accounts banned, and usually unpaid.
  • Pay is usually per 1,000 views, often with a minimum and a cap. Read the current numbers on the campaign page.

Without a program

Clipping a creator who hasn't allowed it can get your posts taken down for copyright and your account banned. Your own streams are always fine. If you're unsure, ask the creator or their manager first.

Honest expectations

Most clips get few views, and many people earn little or nothing at first. What helps: posting fast, posting often, a strong first 3 seconds, and moments that make sense without context.
